Few words cause as much confusion as "atheism". Some people think it is a religion. Others think it means hating God, or believing in nothing at all. In this post I will explain what atheism actually means, what it does not mean, why people become atheists, and how believers respond.

## The simple definition

**Atheism is the lack of belief in gods.**

The word comes from the Greek *a-* (without) and *theos* (god). An atheist is a person who does not believe that any god exists. That is all the word itself tells us. It does not say what an atheist believes about morality, politics, science or the meaning of life. Atheists are as varied as any other group of people.

## Atheism vs. agnosticism

These two words answer two different questions.

- **Belief:** Do you believe in a god? (theist or atheist)
- **Knowledge:** Do you claim to know whether a god exists? (gnostic or agnostic)

Because of this, a person can be an **agnostic atheist**: "I do not believe in a god, but I do not claim to know for certain." Many atheists describe themselves this way. A **gnostic atheist** is someone who says "I am confident that no god exists." An **agnostic theist** believes in a god but admits they cannot be sure.

## Different kinds of atheism

- **Explicit (strong) atheism:** actively saying that gods do not exist.
- **Weak (negative) atheism:** simply not holding a belief in any god, without making a claim that none exist.
- **Implicit atheism:** never having thought about the question, for example a small child.
- **Practical atheism:** living as if gods do not matter, even without a formal position.

## What atheism is not

**"Atheism is a religion."** A religion usually has beliefs, rituals, holy texts or a community. Atheism has none of these by definition. It is one answer to one question, in the same way that not collecting stamps is not a hobby.

**"Atheists believe in nothing."** Most atheists believe in many things: science, human rights, family, art, honesty, justice. They simply do not include a god.

**"Atheists have no morals."** Studies in psychology and sociology give no good reason to think that religious people are more moral than non-religious people. Atheists usually root morality in empathy, reason, shared human wellbeing and the laws and customs of their societies. Believers, of course, often root theirs in scripture and faith. Both groups contain good and bad people.

**"Atheists hate God."** Someone cannot hate what they do not think exists. Some atheists are angry at religious institutions or at harm done in the name of religion. Many others feel no hostility at all, and some have close friends and family who are devout.

**"Atheists are certain they are right."** Some are. Many hold their view provisionally, saying they would change their mind with good evidence.

## A short history

Doubt about gods is very old.

- In ancient India, the **Charvaka (Lokayata)** school rejected the supernatural and relied on direct observation.
- In ancient Greece, thinkers such as **Diagoras of Melos** and **Democritus** questioned or rejected the traditional gods.
- In China, some thinkers of the Confucian and Daoist traditions, like **Xunzi** and **Wang Chong**, gave naturalistic explanations of the world.
- Some religions are themselves **non-theistic**. Early Buddhism and Jainism do not center on a creator god.
- In the **European Enlightenment (17th-18th centuries)**, open atheism began to appear in writing, though it was often dangerous to say aloud.
- In the **19th and 20th centuries**, the rise of modern science, Darwin's theory of evolution and growing secular societies made atheism far more public.

## Why do people become atheists?

People give many reasons. Here are the most common ones:

1. **Lack of evidence.** They feel that no convincing evidence for a god exists, and that claims should be supported by evidence.
2. **The problem of evil.** They ask why a loving and all-powerful god would allow so much suffering.
3. **Religious diversity.** Thousands of religions make conflicting claims, and they cannot all be right.
4. **Science.** Natural explanations now cover many things that once had religious explanations.
5. **Personal experience.** Some grew up in a religion and gradually stopped believing after asking questions.

## What believers say in reply

A fair discussion needs the other side too. Many thoughtful theists respond like this:

- **The universe needs a cause or a reason** (the cosmological argument).
- **The universe appears finely tuned** for life (the fine-tuning argument).
- **Moral values and consciousness** are hard to explain without a source beyond nature.
- **Personal religious experience** and community give life meaning that evidence alone cannot measure.
- **The problem of evil** has been answered by many theologians, for example through free will.

Atheists and skeptics reply that these arguments do not prove a specific god, that "we do not know yet" is different from "therefore god", and that science keeps finding natural explanations. The debate is old and is still going on. I encourage you to read the best arguments from both sides before deciding.

## Being an atheist in the real world

Atheism is not equally accepted everywhere. In many countries, atheists can speak freely. In others they face social pressure, family rejection, job discrimination or even legal penalties for leaving or criticizing a religion. Organizations such as Humanists International track these problems. Whatever our views, freedom of thought and conscience, which includes the freedom to believe and the freedom not to believe, is a basic human right.

## Final thoughts

Atheism, at its core, is a very small idea: not believing in gods. What people build on top of that is up to them. Some become humanists, some become skeptics, some simply live quietly. What matters most is that we ask honest questions, follow the evidence and treat each other with respect.
