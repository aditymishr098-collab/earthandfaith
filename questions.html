These are questions that people often ask about religion, atheism and doubt, with my honest answers. They are the common questions, not messages sent by readers. When readers send good questions through the Ask page, I add the answer here without your name or email. My answers are personal opinion backed by facts where I can. They are not professional advice.

Is atheism a religion?

No. A religion usually has beliefs about the divine, rituals, texts or a community. Atheism is only the absence of belief in gods. Saying that not collecting stamps is a hobby would be about as accurate. Atheists have no shared book, leader or practice, and they disagree on many things.

Do atheists believe in nothing?

No. Most atheists believe in a lot: honesty, family, justice, science, kindness, nature. What they do not accept is gods and the supernatural. I do not follow any religion myself, and I still believe in doing no harm and in looking after this planet.

Can a person be good without religion?

Yes, and history shows that religious people can also do terrible things. Kindness comes from empathy, upbringing, law and community. It does not come from one source only. Religion has inspired great care and also great cruelty, and the lack of religion has done the same. I look at what a person does, not what label they carry. I wrote about this in more detail under Topics in the post on religion and violence.

What is the difference between an atheist and an agnostic?

An atheist does not believe in any god. An agnostic says we cannot know. They answer different questions, belief and knowledge, so a person can be both: someone who does not believe and also admits that certainty is impossible. Many people who call themselves agnostic would also be called atheist in practice. See the Glossary.

Does science disprove God?

Not exactly. Science studies what can be tested. It can show that a claim about nature is wrong, for example that disease is caused by curses, or that the Earth is 6,000 years old. A vague idea of a god that never touches the world cannot be tested at all. So science does not "disprove God", but it has made many specific religious claims about the natural world very hard to defend.

Isn't doubting religion just a Western idea?

No. The Charvaka school in India questioned an afterlife and rituals more than 2,000 years ago. Buddhism and Jainism rejected a creator god. In modern times Periyar, Gora and Bhagat Singh argued openly against blind belief. See Thinkers.

If you say you do not hate anyone, why criticise religion at all?

Because ideas affect real lives, and ideas should be open to questions. I criticise claims, practices and laws, not the people who hold them. A believer who reads my posts and disagrees is welcome. If a post sounds like it insults a person instead of an idea, please tell me.

Did religion cause most wars?

No. One well-known survey of 1,763 conflicts classed only about 7% as religious wars. Land, power, money and fear cause most wars. But religion can turn an ordinary dispute into a holy one, and that makes it harder to stop. Look at the full numbers in the post on the topic, and note that the estimates are rough.

Is it wrong for a non-believer to join festivals or visit temples?

I don't think so. Festivals are also culture, food, family and memory. Many non-believers join in because they enjoy being with people they love. The one thing I would avoid is pretending to believe in a way that hurts you or lies to others. That is your own call.

Should I leave my religion?

That is your decision, and I will not tell you what to do. I would only ask you to think freely, read widely, and not to decide out of fear or anger, in either direction. Whatever you choose, please do not hate the people you leave behind. If your family or safety is involved, talk to someone you trust.
