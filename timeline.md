A quick timeline showing when major religions and movements began, one line each.

**Please read this first.** Dates like these are approximate. Many religions had no single founder or start date, they grew slowly over centuries. For ancient traditions, historians often disagree by hundreds of years. "c." means "around". This page is a starting point, not a final answer.

## Before written history

- **c. 100,000 years ago:** Some of the earliest known burials with grave goods, which some researchers see as a sign of early beliefs about death. The meaning is debated.
- **c. 9500 BCE:** Göbekli Tepe in modern Turkey, one of the oldest known monumental sites, probably built for gatherings or ritual. Its exact purpose is still argued.

## Ancient civilizations

- **c. 3500-3000 BCE:** Sumerian religion in Mesopotamia, with gods of sky, earth and water, and early temples.
- **c. 3100 BCE:** Ancient Egyptian religion takes shape around the pharaohs, Ra, Osiris and Isis.
- **c. 1500-1200 BCE:** Vedic religion in India, with hymns of the Rigveda, forming the early roots of Hinduism.
- **c. 1500-600 BCE:** Zoroastrianism in Persia, founded by the prophet Zoroaster. His dates are strongly disputed.
- **c. 1200-600 BCE:** Israelite religion develops into Judaism, with its scriptures gathered and edited over many centuries.
- **c. 800-400 BCE:** Greek religion and later Roman religion, with the Olympian gods, temples and mystery cults.

## The age of great teachers

- **c. 6th century BCE:** Taoism is traditionally linked to Laozi, though whether he was a real person is debated.
- **c. 6th-5th century BCE:** Jainism spreads through the teaching of Mahavira, who followed earlier teachers.
- **c. 5th century BCE:** Buddhism begins with Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha. The exact dates of his life are debated.
- **c. 551-479 BCE:** Confucius teaches in China, and Confucianism grows from his ideas.
- **c. 500 BCE-500 CE:** Hinduism takes its later shape as texts like the Upanishads, the Mahabharata and the Bhagavad Gita appear. It has no single founder.

## The rise of world religions

- **c. 30 CE:** Christianity begins in the life and teaching of Jesus of Nazareth and spreads across the Roman Empire.
- **c. 1st century CE:** Buddhism reaches China along trade routes.
- **c. 3rd century CE:** Manichaeism is founded by Mani in Persia. It spread widely but later disappeared.
- **325 CE:** The Council of Nicaea, an early gathering of Christian leaders to settle disputes over belief.
- **610-632 CE:** Islam begins with the teachings of Muhammad in Arabia. Sunni and Shia branches emerge after his death in 632.
- **8th century CE:** The oldest written records of Shinto in Japan (the Kojiki, 712 CE). Its practices are much older.

## Splits and new movements

- **1054:** The Great Schism separates Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic Christianity.
- **c. 1500:** Sikhism begins with Guru Nanak in the Punjab region.
- **1517:** The Protestant Reformation begins in Europe, leading to Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican and other churches.
- **1830:** The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Mormonism) is founded in the United States.
- **1844-1863:** The Baháʼí Faith emerges in Persia from the teachings of the Báb and Baháʼu'lláh.
- **1875:** The Theosophical Society is founded in New York, blending ideas from Eastern and Western traditions.
- **1889:** The Ahmadiyya movement begins in India.
- **1926:** Cao Dai is founded in Vietnam, mixing elements of several religions.
- **1930s:** Rastafari grows in Jamaica.
- **1954:** The Church of Scientology is founded. The same year, Gerald Gardner publishes a book on witchcraft that helped start modern Wicca.
- **2005:** Pastafarianism appears as a satirical response to a debate about teaching creationism in American schools.

## What this timeline cannot show

- **Older than it looks.** Many Indigenous and folk traditions in Africa, the Americas, Australia and the Pacific go back tens of thousands of years, but they have no clear start date, so they do not appear here.
- **Branches, not just beginnings.** Every big religion has many branches, and new ones keep appearing.
- **"Founded" is fuzzy.** Some religions, like Hinduism and Shinto, have no founder. In others, believers and historians disagree about when things began.
