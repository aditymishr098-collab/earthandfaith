50 words about belief, doubt and science, explained in plain English. I try to keep each meaning fair to every side. You can type in the box to search.

A B C D E F H K M N O P R S T W
Agnosticism
The view that we cannot know whether God exists, or that the evidence is not enough to decide. The word was coined by T. H. Huxley in 1869. An agnostic neither claims God exists nor claims God does not.
Animism
The belief that animals, plants, rivers, mountains and other natural things have a spirit. It is found in many tribal and indigenous traditions around the world.
Apostasy
Leaving or openly rejecting the religion you were born into. In some countries it is punished by law. On this site it is treated as a personal choice.
Atheism
Not believing in any god. It is not a religion and has no holy book or leader. Atheists disagree with each other about many things. See also the post What Is Atheism under Topics.
Belief
Accepting something as true. It can rest on evidence, on trust in someone, or on faith. The words are often used loosely, which causes confusion.
Blasphemy
Speech or action that is seen as insulting to a god or a religion. Several countries still have blasphemy laws, and accusations have led to mob killings.
Burden of proof
The idea that the person who makes a claim must give the evidence for it, and that others do not have to disprove it. Bertrand Russell's teapot example is the famous illustration.
Charvaka
An ancient Indian school of thought that accepted only perception as a source of knowledge and rejected an afterlife and rituals for the dead. Also called Lokayata. See Thinkers.
Communalism
In South Asia, the use of religious identity for politics, which sets one religious community against another. It is different from being religious. It is also not the same as "community" in everyday English.
Cult
A small religious or devotional group, usually built around one leader, with strong control over its members. The word is often used as an insult, so historians sometimes prefer "new religious movement".
Deism
The belief that a creator made the universe but does not interfere in it, and does not answer prayers or send holy books. Popular among some thinkers of the 1700s.
Denomination
A large branch inside a religion, such as Catholic and Protestant in Christianity, or Sunni and Shia in Islam.
Dharma
A word with many meanings in Indian traditions: duty, right conduct, law, or a religion's teaching. In Buddhism it means the Buddha's teaching. It does not translate neatly as "religion".
Dogma
A teaching that a religion or group requires its members to accept without question. The word is also used for any rigid belief.
Empiricism
The idea that knowledge comes from experience and observation. It is the foundation of modern science.
Extremism
Holding an idea so strongly that you accept violence or hatred against others for it. It can be religious, political or nationalist.
Faith
Trust or confidence, often in something unseen. Believers use it positively, as trust and hope. Critics use it for belief without enough evidence. People mean different things by it.
Falsifiability
The idea that a scientific claim must be testable in a way that could prove it wrong. If nothing could ever prove a claim wrong, it is not science. Karl Popper made this idea famous.
Free thinker
Someone who forms opinions through their own reasoning, not through tradition or authority. Often used for people who question religion.
Fundamentalism
A strict, literal approach to a religion's texts and rules, often in reaction to modern change. It exists in many religions. Fundamentalists are not always violent.
Heresy
A belief that goes against what a religion's authorities say is correct. What counts as heresy depends on who holds the power. Many heretics were later accepted.
Humanism
A way of living based on reason, compassion and human dignity, without needing God. Some humanists are non-religious, and some religious people also call themselves humanists.
Karma
In Indian traditions, the idea that actions have consequences that follow a being, including into future lives. Different religions explain it in different ways.
Materialism
The view that everything that exists is matter and energy, and that mind and consciousness come from the brain. Also, in everyday speech, caring too much about owning things, which is a different meaning.
Miracle
An event said to break the laws of nature through divine power. Science can only test miracle claims that leave evidence, and most reported miracles have natural or unclear explanations.
Moksha
In several Indian traditions, release from the cycle of birth and death. Jains, Hindus and Sikhs understand it differently.
Monotheism
Belief in one God. Judaism, Christianity, Islam and Sikhism are monotheistic. Some Hindu traditions are too, while others are not.
Naturalism
The view that nature is all there is, and that everything can be studied by science. There is no supernatural realm.
Nirvana
In Buddhism, the end of craving and suffering. In Jainism and some Hindu traditions it has related but different meanings. It is not "heaven".
Orthodoxy
Holding to the officially accepted teaching. With a capital O, it also names the Eastern Orthodox Church.
Pantheism
The belief that God and the universe are the same thing, so that nature itself is divine. Spinoza is the best-known pantheist in European thought.
Pluralism
Accepting that many religions and ways of life can exist side by side in one society, with equal rights.
Polytheism
Belief in many gods. Ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome were polytheistic, and so are many forms of Hinduism today.
Proselytism
Trying to convince others to join your religion. It is protected in many countries, but forced or bribed conversion is wrong in any country.
Pseudoscience
Claims that look scientific but do not follow the scientific method. Typical signs: no testing, no willingness to be disproved, and heavy use of impressive words.
Rationalism
Giving priority to reason, logic and evidence over tradition, emotion or authority. In India, "rationalist" usually means a person who opposes superstition.
Reincarnation
The belief that after death a being is born again in another body. It is found in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism and some other traditions.
Ritual
A set of actions done in a fixed way for a religious or social purpose, such as prayer, fasting or a wedding ceremony.
Sanatan Dharma
A term meaning "eternal order". Many Hindus use it for Hinduism as a timeless way of life and duty. Its meaning and use are debated in India today, and different people use it in different ways. I use it only to explain the word, not to take a side.
Scepticism
Doubting claims until there is good evidence. It does not mean rejecting everything. A sceptic asks "how do you know?" and is ready to accept good answers.
Scientific temper
A phrase from India's Constitution (Article 51A) that asks citizens to develop a scientific attitude, to question, and to be open to reform.
Sect
A smaller group that splits from a larger religion. Like "cult", the word can sound negative, so it is used carefully.
Secular state
A government that does not officially favour any religion and treats all citizens equally, believers and non-believers alike. It differs from being anti-religious.
Secularism
The principle that government and religion should be kept separate. In the West it often means a strict separation. In India it means the state treats all religions equally and may engage with them.
Shamanism
Practices in which a person, the shaman, claims to talk with spirits, often for healing. It is found among many peoples, including in Siberia and the Americas.
Superstition
A belief or practice based on fear or luck, with no real evidence for it, such as the idea that a black cat brings bad luck. Whether a religious practice is "superstition" is argued over, and the word can be unfair.
Theism
Belief in at least one god. The opposite of atheism.
Theocracy
A system of government ruled by religious leaders or religious law. Iran and the Vatican City are two modern examples.
Tolerance
Letting others live by their beliefs, even if you disagree. It is a minimum: respecting people is better than only putting up with them.
Worldview
The overall way a person understands life, the universe and what matters. Religious and non-religious people both have one.
