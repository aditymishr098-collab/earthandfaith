"How many religions are there in the world?" sounds like a simple question. It is not. There is no single agreed answer, because it depends on what we call a "religion" and what we call a separate one. Is Catholicism a different religion from Protestantism? Are Sunni and Shia Islam two religions or one? Some scholars and researchers estimate that there are thousands of religions and belief systems if we count every tradition, tribal belief and new movement. Nobody can produce a perfect list.

What I can do is give you the widest map I can: every major family of belief, and as many individual religions, traditions and movements as I can name. Use it as a starting point for your own reading.

## The big picture

According to Pew Research Center estimates for 2020, the world's population divides roughly like this:

- **Christianity:** about 2.3 billion people
- **Islam:** about 1.9 billion
- **No religion (unaffiliated):** about 1.9 billion
- **Hinduism:** about 1.2 billion
- **Buddhism:** about 320 million
- **Folk and traditional religions:** about 400 million
- **Everything else** (Sikhism, Judaism, Baháʼí Faith, Jainism, Shinto, Taoism and many more): tens of millions combined

These numbers are estimates and change every year, but they show how large the gap is between a few giant traditions and a very long tail of smaller ones.

## 1. Abrahamic religions

These traditions trace their roots to the figure of Abraham.

- **Judaism**, with branches such as Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, Reconstructionist and Karaite Judaism
- **Christianity**, with major branches: Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Protestant (Lutheran, Reformed/Presbyterian, Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, Pentecostal and thousands of others), and the Assyrian Church of the East
- **Islam**, with branches such as Sunni, Shia and Ibadi, and movements such as Sufism, Ahmadiyya and Alevism
- **Baháʼí Faith**
- **Druze faith**
- **Samaritanism**
- **Mandaeism**
- **Yazidism**
- **Rastafari** (a movement that grew out of Christianity and Jamaican culture)

## 2. Indian (Dharmic) religions

- **Hinduism**, with traditions such as Vaishnavism, Shaivism, Shaktism and Smartism, plus reform movements like Arya Samaj, Brahmo Samaj and Swaminarayan
- **Buddhism**, with Theravada, Mahayana (Zen, Pure Land, Nichiren and others) and Vajrayana (Tibetan) branches
- **Jainism**, with Digambara and Svetambara traditions
- **Sikhism**
- **Ravidassia**
- **Lingayatism (Veerashaivism)**
- **Ayyavazhi**
- **Kabir Panth**
- **Sanamahism** (Manipur)
- **Sarna Dharma** and other tribal faiths of central and eastern India
- **Donyi-Polo** (Arunachal Pradesh)
- **Kirat Mundhum** (Nepal)
- **Bön** (Tibet)

## 3. East Asian religions

- **Confucianism**
- **Taoism (Daoism)**
- **Chinese folk religion**
- **Shinto** (Japan)
- **Korean shamanism (Muism)**
- **Vietnamese folk religion**, including the worship of the Mother Goddesses
- **Cao Dai** and **Hoa Hao** (Vietnam)
- **Tenrikyo**, **Konkokyo**, **Oomoto**, **Seicho-no-Ie** and **Soka Gakkai** (Japan)
- **Cheondogyo**, **Jeungsanism** and **Won Buddhism** (Korea)
- **Falun Gong** and **Yiguandao** (China and Taiwan)

## 4. Iranian and ancient Near Eastern religions

- **Zoroastrianism** (also called Parsi or Mazdayasna), still practiced today
- **Yarsanism** (Ahl-e Haqq)
- **Manichaeism** (once widespread, now extinct)
- **Mithraism** (ancient Roman mystery religion)
- **Gnosticism** (ancient movements, with echoes today)
- **Ancient Mesopotamian, Canaanite and Egyptian religions**

## 5. Indigenous and traditional religions

Most of the world's peoples had their own belief systems long before the large religions spread. Many still do.

- **Native American and First Nations religions** (hundreds of distinct nations and traditions, including Lakota, Navajo, Hopi, Cherokee, Iroquois and many others)
- **Inuit** and **Arctic** belief systems
- **Australian Aboriginal religions** (often called the Dreaming)
- **Māori** and other **Polynesian, Melanesian and Micronesian** religions
- **Siberian shamanism**, **Tengrism** (Turkic and Mongolic peoples) and **Sámi** traditional religion
- **Ainu** religion (Japan)
- **Amazonian** and **Andean** indigenous religions
- **Ancient Aztec, Maya and Inca religions**, now mostly historical, with modern revivals and blended forms

## 6. African traditional religions

Africa has hundreds of ethnic traditions. Well-known examples include:

- **Yoruba religion** (Ifá and the Orishas)
- **Akan** religion
- **Igbo** religion (Odinani)
- **Zulu** and other **Bantu** traditional beliefs
- **Dinka**, **Serer**, **San** and many more

## 7. African diaspora religions

These grew in the Americas from African traditions mixed with other influences.

- **Haitian Vodou**
- **Santería (Lucumí)**
- **Candomblé** and **Umbanda** (Brazil)
- **Palo** (Cuba)
- **Obeah** (Caribbean)
- **Winti** (Suriname)

## 8. European and revived ancient religions

- **Ancient Greek, Roman, Norse, Celtic and Slavic religions** (historical)
- **Modern Paganism (Neopaganism)**, including **Wicca**, **Druidry**, **Heathenry / Ásatrú**, **Hellenism (Hellenismos)**, **Slavic Rodnovery**, **Romuva** (Lithuania) and **Kemetism** (modern Egyptian revival)

## 9. New religious movements

- **Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints** (Mormonism)
- **Jehovah's Witnesses**
- **Seventh-day Adventism**
- **Christian Science**
- **Unification Church**
- **Scientology**
- **Raëlism**
- **Eckankar**
- **Theosophy** and **Anthroposophy**
- **Spiritism**
- **Thelema**
- **Unitarian Universalism**
- **Subud**
- **Sukyo Mahikari**

## 10. Parody and satirical religions

Some groups began as jokes or social commentary, though a few members take them seriously.

- **Pastafarianism** (Church of the Flying Spaghetti Monster)
- **Discordianism**
- **Jediism**
- **The Satanic Temple** and **LaVeyan Satanism** (both are largely non-theistic and focus on rights, individualism and symbolism)

## 11. Non-religious worldviews

Not everyone believes in a god, and these views are part of the picture too.

- **Atheism**
- **Agnosticism**
- **Humanism**
- **Deism**
- **Pantheism**
- **Secularism**

## Why the number is so hard to pin down

1. **Definition problem.** Some scholars treat Confucianism as a philosophy, not a religion. Others do the opposite.
2. **Branches vs. religions.** Thousands of Christian denominations exist. If each counts as a religion, the total explodes.
3. **Blended beliefs.** Many people practice more than one tradition. This is common in China, Japan and Vietnam.
4. **Small and oral traditions.** Some belief systems are held by only a few hundred people and were never written down.
5. **New movements appear and old ones fade.** Any list is out of date the moment it is published.

## Final thoughts

Whatever we believe, the sheer variety of religions shows how deeply humans search for meaning, purpose and explanations. Looking at them side by side also raises useful questions. Why do so many traditions contradict each other? What do they have in common? How do we decide which claims are supported by evidence?
